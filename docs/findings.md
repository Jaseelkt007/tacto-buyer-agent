## 3. P02 deep dive (the worst case: 0.499)

### The case
Nordmark (large group, sole source for the 7.5 kW motor, 6,110 a year, horizon 1 year) asks +6.5 %:
€348.32 → €370.96 from 2027-01-01, because copper rose 47.7 % and is 20 % of its cost. Negotiable: price,
payment days (14-90), a copper index clause (share 20 %, deadband 0 / 3 / 5 %). Fixed: 12 months,
35-day delivery, DAP. Goal **stabilise** (relationship charge applies). Plan B: a quote at €421.06.
Committee: price moves above its key account manager's band need pricing-committee approval.

### What happened in our runs (9 unique runs)
- **Without a clause the supplier never goes below 1.002 of its opening.** Our 90-day payment ask
  pushes its first counter to 1.006-1.008 of its opening; it slides to 1.002-1.004, final offer, we
  accept: **0.499-0.507**.
- **v1.4 offered the clause (5 % deadband) at €353.14 and it agreed at once**, but that was a second
  sheet priced to cost us the same in cash (relationship charge ignored): relationship charge **€103,819**,
  index risk €17,261, **score 0.365**.
- From v1.5 the health-floor rule suppressed every clause sheet in P02; v1.7 also switched the proactive
  clause off for strategic goals. **From v1.5 on, the one trade that works was never offered.**
- The reference result: clause (5 % deadband) in round 1 at about €361.67, agreed at once, **0.735**.
- At its final offer the supplier wrote "Below this price we would need facts, not just a lower
  number", and its back office: "The buyer wants more than my band allows … Approve a further step?"

### C_open (arena: €2,282,867)
| part | € |
|---|---|
| price 6,110 × 370.96 | 2,266,566 |
| payment interest (its 30 days vs our 60) | 15,110 |
| quality | 1,191 |
| relationship | 0 (its margin at its own quote is +0.64 %, above the sustainable 0.5 %) |

Our engine's C_open is €2,290,792: our too-high full-cost estimate invents a €7,924 relationship charge.
C_best = €2,235,514, so the whole score range of P02 is **€47,353**: every €1/unit = about €6,100 = 0.13
score points.

### The relationship charge and the health price
`charge = €31,920 per margin point × soft(0.5 − its margin) × years`, `soft(g) = g²/10` up to 5 points.
With the arena's real full cost (about **€368.6**, backed out of two runs):

| our price (pay 90) | its margin | charge |
|---|---|---|
| 371.95 | +0.07 % | €596 |
| 365.00 | −1.83 % | €17,402 |
| **361.30** | −2.88 % | €36,419 ← all-in optimum |
| 355.00 | −4.70 % | €86,296 |
| 348.32 (our opening) | −6.71 % | €150,391 |

With the clause at €361.3 the local score is **0.759**. The optimum moves about 1:1 with the full-cost
estimate: **in every strategic case the most sensitive number is the supplier's hidden full cost.**

### Root cause in P02
1. **The economics cannot see the value of risk protection.** The kit's `supplier_value()` (cost.py,
   line 81: "Contract length and an index clause are security, not cash: they do not count here") is
   right for the arena's margin charge but we also used it to predict **what the supplier will accept**.
   Two questions, one function: the clause was invisible.
2. **The stale health price:** €367.46 instead of about €362.
3. **The lever in P02 is a trade, not a fact.** No argument can win it.

## 5. Architecture findings

### Narrow pipes
Each module extracts a fixed slice: `claims.py` 7 argument kinds, `dossier.py` 4 regex signals and 3
cost-source rules, `supplier_value` cash only, `policy.py` fixed constants over those slices. Anything
outside the slices never reaches a decision: a committee band, a need for copper protection, "below this
price we would need facts". The information is in the data, in its e-mails and even in our LLM dossier,
but **no rule consumes it**. (A back-office line like "my band ends at…" is visible only after the case
ends, so it teaches us offline and can never feed a live decision.)

### We reply instantly although we have a minute
| measure (316 moves, v1.6-v1.7) | value |
|---|---|
| our decision time, median | 1 ms |
| 95th percentile | 54 ms |
| slowest | 91 ms |
| wall clock per message (incl. the supplier's reply), median / max | about 14 s / 39 s |

Timeouts: none in single-supplier cases; in the RFQ P05:

| version | timeouts | case duration |
|---|---|---|
| v1.7 | 5 (3 to a dropped bidder, 1 to a contender in round 1, 1 more) + sheetless keepalives | 379 s |
| v1.7b | 2 | 254 s |
| v1.7c | 0 | 88 s |
| v1.7c + case plan | 3 | 201 s |

- In single-supplier cases about 40+ seconds per move are free for thinking; the rule engine uses none.
- In RFQs the arena serialises calls within a session: a thread waits about (bidders − 1) × the reply time
  for the others, so thinking must be **budgeted per thread**, and sometimes we must decide which bidder
  to answer first and how long to think (a point the judges raised). The rule engine only round-robins.
- In the final up to 10 cases run in parallel (separate sessions, not serialised against each other) and
  the 8-minute wave cap applies.

### Wrong context is worse than no context
Several of our rules are unverified (section 4). If their outputs go into an LLM prompt as facts, the
model reasons confidently from wrong premises (seen in zone 1: the playbook's "36 months adds nothing",
my stance definition "soft for strategic suppliers", the dossier's "clause without a deadband"). Every
context item handed to a model must say where it comes from (arena-given, data, our estimate, our rule)
and how certain it is; estimates must carry their range.

## 6. Requirements for the next step (the user's direction)

1. **Hybrid architecture in every module:** the deterministic modules become the context and the tools
   (verified facts with IDs, a priced menu of options with C and supplier value, the health-price curve,
   limits, state); an LLM makes the judgment (which option, how far to move, which argument, what to
   say); code keeps the guard rails (validate, exact prices within bounds, must-haves, final-offer rule,
   accept check, clock).
2. **Every move finishes inside the 60 seconds**, with a time budget per thread (RFQ) and per wave, and a
   coded fallback when the model is late.
3. **Analyse the unverified rules** (section 4) before they feed a model: keep what is arena-given or
   data, mark estimates with their range, replace invented constants where the data allows (e.g. back
   out a supplier's full cost and cost of money from its own behaviour).
4. **Never put wrong context into the LLM:** every item labelled with its source and certainty.
5. **Analyse case by case, worst first** (P02, P08 + P18, P10, P11, P05, then the rest), and test each
   change only on the case it targets first (local scorer, then one hosted run), then across all cases.
6. Keep v1.7c (72.7) as the floor: a layer ships only when it beats it.

## 7. Open questions
- How to estimate a supplier's full cost and cost of money better from its quote, its counters and its
  documents (the most sensitive inputs in strategic cases).
- Which LLM, effort and time budget per call fit the 60-second window, and how to pipeline calls in RFQs.
- How to value non-cash terms (clause protection, contract length, volume security) for "what the
  supplier will accept" without changing the arena's C.
- Whether a fresh verified fact can move a supplier after its final offer (it said so in P02), or only
  ever risks a walk-away (6 of 7 cases in practice).

## 9. Implementation plan (Sun 25 Oct, from 02:00; code freeze 13:00)

### The core problem, and where the model comes in
Information gets lost because deterministic rules are stacked:
- each module passes on a narrow slice of the data;
- fixed constants decide on those slices.

Patching a rule with another rule repeats the problem. The first draft of this plan did exactly that
in step 1.3: "offer the clause in strategic goals, code picks the deadband" was a new rule fitted to
P02. It is removed. The work now splits in two:
- **Code makes the context right and complete.** It fixes wrong numbers (bugs; estimates get a
  range), prices every allowed option, and removes the filters that hide options. **It adds no new
  decision rules.**
- **The model makes the decisions that rules make today:** what the supplier wants, which option,
  how far to move, which argument, what to say, when to stop.

### Where the model decides
| zone | when | call | what it decides | state |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | offline, before the final | dossier per supplier (Opus 5.5, low effort) | observations about the supplier, each with an ID: its needs, pressure, approvals, traps | built (`2168e02`) |
| 1 | case start, before the session opens | case strategist | facts only: contractual trades, traps to avoid | built; flag `case_plan` off (stance and paragraph hurt) |
| 2 + 3 | the opening e-mail and every reply, inside the 60 s | **tactician** (new), one call per move | reads its reply; chooses the action (accept / counter / hold / walk), the option, the price rung and the arguments; writes the e-mail body | phases 2 and 3 |

### Does fixing P02 also fix other cases?
Mostly yes. The P02 causes are in shared code, not in P02's data:

| P02 cause | Where | Cases it touches (21 practice + sandbox files; **hosted**) |
|---|---|---|
| stale health price | `economics.cheapest_price`, called once at setup | every stabilise / secure_supply case: **P01, P02, P03, P10**, P06, P09, P14, P17, D01 (9 of 21) |
| full-cost estimate (99.5 % cap, phantom €7,924 in our C_open) | `dossier.bound_full_cost` | the same 9 |
| clause invisible; proactive clause off for strategic goals | `supplier_value`, `plan_terms`, flag `proactive_clause` | every case with a negotiable index clause: **P02, P08, P18**, P14, P16 (5 of 21) |
| owner-type cost of money | `dossier.OWNER_RATE` | every case that negotiates payment days (18 of 21) |

But a shared fix can also break other cases. v1.4 offered the same P02 clause at the wrong price and
scored 0.365. So each fix is tested on P02 first, then on every case it touches.

The final has new suppliers and new settings. A fix built on the published formula (C, the
relationship charge) carries over to them. A constant tuned on P02 does not. **No P02 number goes into
the code:** no fixed deadband, no fixed price.

The worst-first order matches the shared fixes:
- P08 and P18 are the next-worst cases, and both negotiate an index clause.
- P10 is secure_supply, so it pays the relationship charge.

### How the hybrid works in each module: one judgment call per move
"Hybrid in each module" does **not** mean one LLM call per module. Five calls in a row (read, estimate,
argue, price, write) take 1-2 minutes and break the 60 s. Instead, each module does two things:
- it delivers labelled context, or priced options, into **one judgment call per move**;
- code acts on the call's answer.

The judgment moves to the model. The modules stay code: they are the model's eyes and its calculator.

| module | today (rule) | hybrid: code provides | hybrid: the model decides | audit before the model sees it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `dossier.py`: supplier estimate | full cost via the 99.5 % cap; cost of money from the owner type plus regex premiums; 4 regex signals | full cost and cost of money as E items, each with a range (from the data and from its own counters); the zone-0 dossier's observations, each with an ID | nothing; it reads them | step 1.2: our estimate vs the backed-out truth. The regex signals are R and are dropped; the dossier's observations replace them |
| `claims.py`: arguments | picks the strongest argument kind by our ranking | every checkable argument as a verified sentence with its figure and ID; ours (`for_us`) and its own (`for_them`) | which IDs to cite now (at most 3); which of its arguments to answer | already checked by the arena (0 contradicted so far). Our strength ranking is R and is not passed |
| `economics.py`: prices | one term mix (`plan_terms`); health price computed once | **the menu:** every allowed option, including each clause deadband, priced by C with the relationship charge; the health curve, refreshed; its cash margin per option | which option fits what this supplier wants | steps 1.1 and 1.3. `supplier_value` is shown only as "its cash margin in the arena's formula", never as "what it will accept" |
| `policy.py`: the move | anchor 0.80-0.92, 30 % step, 10 % hold, 45 % stuck, the accept rules | **a price ladder** for each option: a few prices set by code between our last offer and its last offer, none below the health price. Plus the state: round, final offer, its moves | accept / counter / hold / walk; which rung; when to stop | the constants become the fallback engine only (R) |
| reply reading (new) | regexes over its text | its e-mail as the live API returns it: text (stock lines marked), terms, alternative sheets, claim results and its verdict per claim (S); **never the back office or its reasons, which arrive only after the case ends** | what it really asks for, and its stated limits | stock-line detection (section 10) |
| `writer.py` | template | the claim sentences and sheet lines, inserted by code | the e-mail body for the chosen option, **in the same call**, so the words follow the choice | the guard checks: no digits in the model's text, no leaks, no promise outside the sheets |
| RFQ scheduler (new) | round robin | a deadline per thread (the time of its reply + 60 s − a margin − the wait behind the other threads); all open threads think in parallel; the oldest reply is sent first | nothing | nothing |
| guard rails | as today | sheet validity, must-haves, final-offer rule, accept check; on a timeout, the v1.7c move | nothing | nothing |

**Time per move:**

| stage | budget |
|---|---|
| build the packet | under 50 ms |
| one model call (low effort, JSON schema) | hard timeout 25 s in a single-supplier case; in an RFQ, the thread's deadline |
| code checks | under 50 ms |
| fallback | the v1.7c move, which is always ready first |

### What the model never receives
- **Our tuned constants:** anchors, steps, thresholds.
- **Our strength ranking** of the arguments.
- **The regex signals.**
- **The playbook lessons as rules.** Each one comes from a few runs; "36 months adds nothing" turned out
  wrong on hosted data.
- **The dossier's recommendations that contain numbers.** Its "clause without a deadband" was the
  costliest option. Its observations, each with an ID, do go in.

### Principles
1. **Fix a rule before any model reads it.** A bad rule is wrong twice: in the engine and in the
   prompt.
2. **Every context item carries a source label:**

   | label | meaning | goes to the LLM as |
   |---|---|---|
   | A arena | case file, `api.md` formula | fact |
   | D data | a record with an ID in the package | fact, with its error band if it has one (should-cost ±5 %) |
   | E estimate | our estimate, from the data or from the supplier's behaviour | value + range + how we got it |
   | S supplier | what the supplier said in this thread | its claim, not a fact |
   | R rule | our tuned constant or heuristic (anchors, steps, regex signals) | **nothing**: R items stay in the fallback engine |
   | Z zone 0 | the offline model's reading of its records, each line with its IDs | an interpretation; never about its cost, which code estimates (E) |

   The rule engine's own move goes in as one option (E0), labelled a default tuned on practice cases.
   The model keeps it or picks another code-priced option, so the hybrid starts from v1.7c.
3. **Numbers come from code.** The LLM picks among options that code has priced and validated.
4. **Every step sits behind a flag. v1.7c (72.7) is the floor.** A step goes on by default only if it
   beats v1.7c on its target cases without hurting the full set.

### Test ladder for each step
**No hosted run until the offline checks show that the change works.**
1. Unit tests.
2. **Local scorer on P02:** C of each option, and the score of our sheet if the supplier accepts it.
3. **Offline replay.** We take the model's choice at every recorded state of P02 (9 unique runs),
   then of the cases the step touches (P08, P18, P10, P01, P03), and score each choice locally. We
   already know from the recordings how P02 reacts to the clause: it accepted the 5 % clause at once,
   at €353.14 (v1.4) and at about €361.67 (reference run).
4. **Free end-to-end runs:** sandbox D01 and D02 (real supplier brains, no quota), and `arena-sim`
   for timeouts, RFQs and waves.
5. **Only then go hosted:** one P02 run; then the hosted cases the step touches (P08 and P18 twice
   each, because they are noisy); then the full set.

### Phase 1: make the context right and complete (code, about 2 h)
These steps add no decision rules. Each one does one of two things:
- it corrects a number the model will read (a wrong number in the packet is a wrong premise);
- it removes a filter that hides an option.

When to offer what stays the model's decision.

| step | change | check |
|---|---|---|
| 1.1 | **Health price refresh** (math bug): recompute `cheapest_price` every time the full-cost estimate changes. This also fixes the v1.7c fallback. | P02 health price about €362, not €367.46; unit test |
| 1.2 | **Audit full cost and cost of money against the arena.** (a) Back out the real full cost from the relationship charge in past runs of P01, P03, P10 and D01 (the method gave €368.6 for P02). (b) Read the supplier's price per payment term from its alternative sheets. (c) Build a table of our estimate vs the truth per case. (d) Store the measured error as the item's range (label E). (e) Remove the phantom charge from our C_open. | the table goes into this file; P02 C_open = €2,282,867 |

## 10. Rule audit: method and first results

### Method
Each rule or constant in `dossier.py`, `economics.py`, `policy.py`, `claims.py` and `writer.py` goes
into one of six types. The type decides how we check the rule against what the arena revealed in the
recorded runs: about 150 runs in `runs/`, each with the arena's cost breakdown, every supplier offer
and its alternative sheets, the claim results, the back office, and our decisions.

| type | examples | how we check it | ground truth in the runs | verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1. our maths on the arena's formula | C, C_open, health price, accept check | recompute the arena's `c_open`, `c_deal` and every cost-breakdown line for every recorded deal; they must match to the cent, except the relationship line | `result.cost_breakdown` | fix bugs (stale health price, our C_open) |
| 2. estimates of a hidden supplier number | cost of money, full cost | back out the true value from its own behaviour: the rate from alternative sheets that differ only in payment days; the full cost from the arena's relationship charge | `move.alternatives`, `cost_breakdown.relationship` | measure it in the session where possible; before that, a prior with the measured error as its range (label E) |
| 3. our predictions of its behaviour | `their_price_on_our_terms`, the equal-value price, the cash / work / capacity / hedged signals | compare each prediction with its next real offer; compare each signal with its own words, its back office, and how it reacted to the matching trade | `decisions` vs the next offer, back office | report the hit rate; a rule that fails is R and stays out of the packet |
| 4. filters that hide options | health-floor suppression, clause off for strategic goals, `clause_trade` exclusions | list the options hidden in each recorded run and price them with the local scorer | runs + local scorer | the model's menu shows every option; the fallback keeps its filters |
| 5. tactic constants | anchors 0.80-0.92, 30 % step, 10 % hold, 45 % stuck, persona floors, keepalive threshold | none possible: these are a strategy, not facts | none | fallback only; never in the packet |
| 6. arguments | argument kinds, their ranking | the arena's claim results (verified / contradicted) and the supplier's price move after each claim | `claim_results`, next offer | IDs and figures are facts (checked by the arena); our ranking is R |

All checks run offline from `runs/`, with no sessions. They go into one script, `scripts/audit.py`,
so they can rerun after new runs and after the Sunday data release.

### Ground rules for using the audit (no overfitting, no cheating)
1. **No practice supplier's measured number goes into the engine.** No rate table, no full-cost table,
   no "2.2 % for the clause". The final has new suppliers, and a fitted table is overfitting.
2. **What ships is a method** that works for any supplier inside its own session, from what the live
   API returns to us. Example: its rate, read from its own alternative sheets.
3. **Practice numbers are used for two things only:**
   - to measure how wrong a rule is;
   - to set an honest range, labelled "seen in N practice cases".
4. **Live inputs are only what the arena sends during the session:**
   - the case file and the data package;
   - its e-mails, terms, alternative sheets, claim results and signals.

   The back office and the supplier's `reasons` arrive only after a case ends (in the final, after
   the final; `api.md`). **They are offline analysis material and never go into a live packet.**
5. **Tune and test on different cases.** Every method is checked on cases it was not built from.

### Results (`scripts/audit.py`, 169 runs; full tables in `notes/analysis/audit.md`)

**Type 1: our maths.** Every cost line of all 160 closed deals matches the arena to the cent. That
covers price, payment, freight, quality, index clause, index risk and the rest.
- **Our C_open at setup matches the arena in every case except P02:** +€7,924. That is the phantom
  relationship charge from the uncapped should-cost (a bug).
- **The health price at setup is too high in every relationship case:**

| case | ours at setup | true | at stake if it accepted at ours |
|---|---|---|---|
| P02 | €367.46 | €361.42 | 0.205 |
| P01 | €133.37 | €130.57 | 0.129 |
| P03 | €425.44 | €418.13 | 0.049 |
| D01 | €48.58 | €49.03 | 0.011 |

The cause is the type-2 inputs.

To recover a standard term the opening does not show (for example P01's 30-day payment), the audit
uses the allowed value that reproduces the arena's C_open exactly. The engine itself reads the quote
PDF, so this is an audit tool only.

**Type 2: cost of money.** The supplier's own alternative sheets fix its rate exactly. Nordmark (P02)
offered €373.13 at 90 days and €369.02 at 30 days: 6.78 % a year, the same in all 13 pairs.

| case | owner type | our rule | its real rate (pairs) | error |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| P01 | family | 12.50 % (7.5 + 5 cash regex) | 9.83 % (1) | +2.67 pp |
| P02 | group | 5.00 % | 6.78 % (13) | −1.78 pp |
| P03 | group | 5.00 % | 4.15 % (10) | +0.85 pp |
| P04 | family | 7.50 % | 7.43 % (8) | +0.07 pp |
| P07 | family | 7.50 % | 8.82 % (16) | −1.32 pp |
| P08 | group | 5.00 % | 6.74 % (2) | −1.74 pp |
| D01 | - | 7.50 % | 9.47 % (14) | −1.97 pp |

- **Owner type does not predict the rate.** Two "group" suppliers charge 4.15 % and 6.7 %.
- **Method that ships:** read the rate from its first alternative sheet that differs from its main
  sheet only in payment days. Before that, use the range seen so far (about 4-10 %), not one invented
  number.

**Type 2: full cost.** Backed out of the arena's relationship charge in every recorded deal, using the
real rate:

| case | deals | true full cost | our should-cost | quote | after the 99.5 % cap (median per run) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| P01 | 12 | €130.05 | €132.64 (+2.0 %) | €138.55 | €130.54 (+0.4 %) |
| P02 | 9 | €367.49 | €374.95 (+2.0 %) | €370.96 | €369.11 (+0.4 %; one run €356.20) |
| P03 | 11 | €396.60 | €403.21 (+1.7 %) | €438.21 | €403.21 (+1.7 %) |

- **Should-cost is inside the data's ±5 % band** (`company.md`). It ran 1.7-2.0 % high three times,
  but three cases are too few for a correction factor. So the range stays the documented ±5 %.
- **The 99.5 % cap's premise is false.** "A supplier never sells below its full cost": v1.4 closed P02
  at €353.14 with the clause, **below its true full cost of €367.49**. In that run the cap dragged the
  estimate to the floor (€356.20).
- **A weaker premise held 3 of 3 times:** "its opening quote is above its full cost". Method: cap the
  estimate by the opening quote only, never by later prices, and label it as an assumption with its
  track record.
- **Correction to section 3:** P02's real full cost is €367.49, not €368.6. The earlier figure came
  from a guessed rate. The true health price is €361.42.

**Type 3: how it prices other terms.** For every alternative sheet, its price was predicted from its
main sheet with the kit's cash model:

| the sheets differ in | pairs | error with its true rate |
|---|---|---|
| payment days | 66 | ±0.3 %: the cash model is right; only our rate was wrong |
| lead time | 27 | +4.3 to +5.8 %: it charges for faster delivery; the cash model counts 0 |
| index clause deadband (0 % vs 5 %) | 27 | −2.0 to −2.2 %: it asks about 2 % more for the 5 % deadband, i.e. it values protection; the cash model counts 0 |
| ppm commitment | 13 | +1.1 % |
| effective date | 6 | +0.1 to +0.7 % |
| volume, contract months, Incoterm | 1-2 each | 0 to +1.3 % (too few) |

- **This measures the `supplier_value` conflation.** The cash model is right for cash terms. It is
  blind to how a supplier values lead time and the clause.
- **Method that ships:** every alternative sheet a supplier sends is a point where it is indifferent
  between two sheets. Code turns each one into "its own sheets show it asks +x % for y", an S/E item
  of this session. No practice percentage is hard-coded.

**Type 3: regex signals.** Most of the supplier text the regexes see is stock lines that 10-15
different suppliers write. Examples: "A binding higher volume would help us and can improve the
price"; "Faster payment is welcome; we can reflect it in the price". So those lines carry no signal.
A few lines are a supplier's own:
- P11: "Faster payment would help us more than anything";
- P10: "our high capacity utilisation".

The binary regex flags lose that text:
- P08 is flagged "hedged", yet it asked for a 0 % deadband clause itself.
- P02 is flagged with nothing.

**Method that ships:**
- the supplier's own non-stock sentences, verbatim (S);
- stock lines found by frequency across suppliers (a property of the arena's bots, not of one
  supplier);
- its measured rate.

The regex flags become fallback-only (R).

**Type 4: options we never offered** (every run, every engine version):
- **any clause** in P08 and P18;
- the 0 % and 3 % deadbands in P02;
- 12 months and the lower volumes in P03;
- every chargeback option in P11;
- 12 months in P07, P10 and P18.

**Method that ships:** the model's menu lists every allowed option, priced by C.

**Type 6: arguments.** The arena found 1 claim contradicted out of 625, but **54 claims came back
"duplicate"**: we re-sent the same ID and wasted slots. In each reply the supplier says which data
moved it ("it holds and we took it into account" vs "it does not change our calculation"):

| data it names | moved it | no change |
|---|---|---|
| notice period | 11 | 0 |
| quality record | 15 | 2 |
| index change | 48 | 11 |
| should-cost | 32 | 11 |
| volume change | 55 | 23 |
| rival quote | 68 | 54 |
| delivery record | 13 | 38 |
| price history | 0 | 26 |

- **Method that ships:** this verdict is in the live e-mail, so it goes into the packet per claim (S).
- **The duplicates are history.** v1.7's lever schedule already sends each kind once per supplier and
  each ID once per case: 0 duplicates in v1.7, v1.7b and v1.7c, against 3 in the case-plan runs.
- **The practice counts are a prior only,** labelled "12 practice suppliers". Price history still
  goes out in P02 round 1, and it never moved anyone.

### Verdicts

| rule | verdict | replaced by (a method, not a fitted value) |
|---|---|---|
| cost maths (`total_cost`, breakdown) | **keep: exact** | none |
| C_open at setup | **bug** | use the capped estimate at setup (the phantom charge goes) |
| health price computed once | **bug** | recompute when the estimate changes; show the range across the full-cost band |
| cost of money by owner type + premiums | **drop** | its rate from its own alternative sheets; the range seen so far until then |
| full cost: should-cost ±5 % | **keep as E** | none (documented band) |
| full-cost cap by every price it offers | **drop** (premise false) | cap by its opening quote only, labelled as an assumption |
| `supplier_value` as "what it will accept" | **drop for that use** | keep it for the arena's margin; acceptance from its own sheet pairs (S/E) |
| regex signals | **fallback only (R)** | its own non-stock sentences, verbatim |
| option filters (health floor, clause off for strategic goals) | **fallback only** | the full priced menu for the model |
| claim ranking | **fallback only (R)** | its per-claim verdict read live; practice counts as a labelled prior |
| anchors, steps, holds | **fallback only (R)** | the model's judgment over the menu |

  - P02 health price €362.86 (true €361.4);
